### Changed

- **`server.telemetry.export_audio` now also sends each turn's clips, which widens the audio class it exports** (#496). With the flag on, the upload after a session closes still sends the session WAV and its manifest to the session's trace first, and then, for every turn the capture kept clips for, the turn's heard clip (the exact audio its speech recognition was handed) and, when reply audio was paced, its reply clip, each attached to that turn's own trace and referenced there by a `capture` span under the turn. A session's audio therefore leaves as the pair plus up to two clips per turn, where it used to leave once. **An operator who agreed to the pair has not agreed to this: re-read the flag's description before upgrading with it on.** Nothing new is sent without the flag, the refusals are unchanged, and the clips go to the same presigned destination the WAV goes to. The flag still sends audio and its metadata only: a turn's trace holds its clips, its transcript and the speech recognition model together only with `server.conversations` storing text and `export_transcripts` on as well, and neither flag implies the other. `capture_uploaded` gains `clips`, the clips attached to their turns, and a new warning, `capture_clips_incomplete`, says once per job how many clips were attached, had no turn to be filed on, failed or were never tried after the backend went away, with the first failure's reason; both reach the session's trace under `vinga.export.*`. The worker reads only through a descriptor whose device, inode and size match what was staged, opened without following links, so a path, a link, a rename or a replaced file in a staged job cannot steer it to another file; the recording's own staged pair is read the same way. It does not defend against a same-size file taking over a staged file's inode after the capture's own names are pruned, which only a writer running as this server's user can arrange, and it claims nothing about the files' content.
