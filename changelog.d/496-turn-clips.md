### Changed

- **A capture now also keeps each turn's heard and reply audio** (#496). Beside a session's three files, `server.capture` writes a `<session>.turns/` directory with mono 16 kHz clips for every turn the session started, a heard clip and, when reply audio was paced, a reply clip, named by the utterance id its `turn_started` event carries: `<utterance>.heard.wav`, the exact audio the turn's speech recognition was handed (for a confirmed barge-in, the audio the barge-in gate transcribed, which the turn reuses), and `<utterance>.reply.wav`, channel 1 of the session WAV over the stretch that turn's reply was paced out, silences and barge-in truncation included. A turn that spoke nothing has no reply clip, and a candidate the barge-in gate turned away is not a turn and has no clips. The manifest written at close lists the turns in start order as `capture.turns`, with each reply clip's span as `reply_from_ms` and `reply_to_ms` on the WAV's `t_ms` timeline, exact to the sample, so cutting channel 1 at those offsets reproduces the clip. The clips count against `max_total_mb` and are pruned with their capture; being mono, they add up to about half the WAV again. Nothing about what `export_audio` uploads changes: it still sends the session WAV and the manifest only.

### Fixed

- **The capture budget now counts a recording staged for upload until the upload has finished with it** (#496). With `server.telemetry.export_audio` on, a closed capture's WAV and manifest are hard-linked into `upload-staging/` under the capture directory while they wait for the uploader. The budget counted only the top level of the directory, so once a prune removed the capture's own names, the staged links kept its blocks on the disk while the budget no longer saw them, and a slow or unreachable backend could hold a backlog of recordings outside `max_total_mb`. The budget now counts every file under the directory, staging included, once per inode, so a staged link and the capture it came from count once while both exist and still count after the prune. The prune still never removes a staged upload, so a backlog over budget is reported by the existing `capture_over_budget` warning, and it drains as the uploads finish. With such a backlog the prune can now remove older captures sooner than before: those bytes were on the disk all along, and the budget is being honoured rather than costing anything new.
